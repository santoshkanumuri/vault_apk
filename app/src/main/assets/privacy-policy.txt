Private Vault privacy policy
Effective date: September 19, 2026

Developer: Santosh Kanumuri
Contact: santosh.kanumuri@hotmail.com

What stays on your device
Private Vault stores the cards, passwords, authenticator setup keys, security questions, notes, groups, folders, app links, and photos you choose to save. Vault records and photos are encrypted in the app's private storage. The master password protects the vault key; the app does not store your master password. Appearance and optional-feature preferences are stored locally.

The app has no Internet permission, server account, advertising, analytics, or developer-operated cloud sync. It does not send your vault contents or app usage history to the developer. Processing inside the app takes place on your device.

Optional permissions
Camera access is used to scan authenticator setup QR codes or capture photos you choose to save. The QR scanner does not save camera frames. Photo capture may open your camera app, whose own privacy practices apply. Imported photo originals remain outside the vault.

NFC is off by default. If enabled, an explicit card scan reads supported card details for you to review and save. It does not make payments or read a card's printed CVV.

Strong biometric authentication is handled by Android. Private Vault does not receive or store fingerprint images or biometric templates. A device-protected, encrypted vault key can enable biometric access for 24 hours after master-password authentication and biometric confirmation.

Optional app suggestions require Usage access, which you grant in Android Settings. When enabled, opening the Vault codes picker checks up to five minutes of recent app activity to suggest linked accounts. No app-usage history is stored or transmitted. The app selector lists launchable apps to let you create links. Those links are saved in your encrypted vault. You can disable suggestions in Private Vault and revoke Usage access in Android Settings.

Backups and sharing you request
Optional password autofill uses Android's Autofill service, which you must select in Android Settings. It inspects form structure to identify supported login or authenticator-code fields. After unlocking and selecting an account, the app sends the chosen username/password or linked code to the authorized app or website through Android, without using the clipboard. The receiving app, browser, and filled website can read those values. App package names and signing identities are saved in the encrypted vault to restrict native-app filling. Website filling checks supported browser release certificates and the exact HTTPS origin saved with the login. Keyboard suggestions show a generic app name and unlock action before authentication.

Supported signup and password-change forms can receive a locally generated password after authentication and confirmation. The app saves it as a separate encrypted login before filling, so it remains recoverable if the browser does not offer a save prompt. Your current login stays unchanged. Submit the website form yourself and check that it accepted the new password before removing an older login.

On Android 14 or newer, you can enable Private Vault as a passkey provider. Supported website requests require a verified browser and exact website host. Native app requests use Android's calling-app signing identity as their origin; the app name and requested site are shown before you confirm. Your vault must be unlocked to create or use a passkey. Passkey private keys stay in the encrypted vault and are included in authenticated encrypted backups. The requesting app or website receives a public credential or a signature, never the private key. Passkey account labels appear after unlocking. Some apps may require their own provider or passkey features that Private Vault does not support. Related or parent-domain browser requests are not supported. Deleting a passkey from the vault does not delete its registration or remove it from older backup files.

For supported browser login forms, Android can offer to save the login. After you accept that prompt, Private Vault temporarily holds the submitted username and password in process memory for up to two minutes while you unlock and review it. It saves or updates the encrypted record only after your confirmation. Cancellation or expiry clears the pending password. No plaintext form file is created. Username-first filling checks each page separately and does not carry passwords to a different website.

Optional password import reads the CSV or unencrypted Bitwarden JSON file you select, previews account names, and saves confirmed entries in the encrypted vault. Private Vault does not keep a plaintext copy of the imported file. The original export contains readable passwords and remains outside the vault until you delete it.

Manual exports create an encrypted backup containing your records, photos, and vault settings. If you choose a cloud document provider, that provider may upload the encrypted file under its own terms. Private Vault does not upload it itself. Backups require the password used when they were exported, even after you change the current vault password.

Copying a field places its value on Android's clipboard so you can paste it into another app. Copied secrets are marked sensitive, and the app attempts to clear its own clipboard item after 30 seconds. Android can prevent background clipboard access, so clearing is not guaranteed. Your keyboard and the receiving app may handle pasted information under their own privacy practices.

Retention and deletion
Your data stays in the vault until you delete it, replace the vault during restore, clear the app's storage, or uninstall the app. Deleting an entry also removes its associated encrypted photo files. Exported backups, imported originals, and copies pasted elsewhere are not deleted with the vault; remove them separately. Logical deletion does not guarantee forensic erasure from flash storage. There is no account-recovery service or master-password reset.

Security limits
The app uses encryption, blocks ordinary screenshots, and locks access when required by its settings. These protections cannot guarantee security on a compromised operating system or against someone who has your master password. The app has not undergone an independent security audit.

Support and policy changes
If you email support, your email provider and the developer's email provider process the message. Do not send vault passwords, card numbers, authenticator setup keys, or backups.
